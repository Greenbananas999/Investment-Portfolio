Forward-looking

Expected annual return by method

Each method answers the same question differently. CAPM prices risk (beta), the DCF solves for the return that today's price implies, Grinold-Kroner adds up dividend yield, growth, buybacks and valuation change, and bonds start from their yield. The consensus is the plain average of the methods. USD figures add the expected currency move from relative purchasing power parity (the inflation gap against the dollar).

Discounted cash flow

DCF value, implied return and input uncertainty

Year-1 cash flow is forward net income (market cap ÷ forward P/E) times each company's own free-cash-flow conversion over its last four fiscal years. Cash flow then grows at the analyst next-year rate (capped at 20%) for four years and fades to terminal growth by year 10. SK Hynix and Samsung trade on peak-cycle earnings (forward P/E near 3), so their cash flow falls to half of year 1 by year 4 before growing with the economy. The implied return is the discount rate at which the DCF equals today's enterprise value. The Monte Carlo redraws growth, terminal growth, WACC, the starting cash flow and, for the memory makers, how far the cycle falls.

Building blocks

Grinold-Kroner decomposition

Expected return = dividend yield + inflation + real earnings growth − net share issuance + repricing. Repricing assumes the forward P/E drifts to a target over ten years; targets are judgement calls listed in the assumptions.

Fixed income

Government bonds: yield, scenarios and the currency drag

For a bond held to the horizon, the yield is most of the expected return; the rest is the price change when yields move. The base case moves yields halfway toward a fair value of a real yield (1.2% for France, 4% for Turkey) plus expected inflation. The dollar return then depends on the currency, and for the lira that is the whole story.

Testing the methods

How well did each method forecast the next year?

Each December from 2015 to 2024, every method forecast the next 12 months' USD return using only data available at the time. Rank correlation asks whether a method at least put the assets in the right order (1 is perfect, 0 is a coin toss). DCF and Grinold-Kroner need point-in-time fundamentals that Yahoo does not keep, so they could not be tested this way.
