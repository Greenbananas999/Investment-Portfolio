1. The pre-registered method

committed before any candidate was backtested. Nothing below may be changed after results are seen except through the change log at the end, and any logged change forces a rerun of every candidate under the new rule.

Project: AFMBA622 Global Portfolio Research, UMass Boston. Client brief: $100,000, 7-year horizon, growth plus security, mostly passive with periodic adjustments, quantitative (fundamental plus technical), at least 70% non-US, roughly 55% stocks / 35% bonds / 10% other, derivatives allowed.

1. Objective in numbers
ItemRule

Horizon7 years from the report date (Nov 2026 to Nov 2033)

If the optimized portfolio cannot meet both the growth target and the security budget, the security budget wins and the shortfall is reported.

2. Sample split and minimum history
  • In-sample: 2010-01-01 to 2017-12-31. All screens, signal tests, and the shortlist use only data before 2018-01-01.
  • Out-of-sample (OOS): 2018-01-01 to the last available month (Oct 2026). Used once, to judge candidates and signals. The walk-forward portfolio backtest re-estimates on a trailing window, so every portfolio return is OOS by construction.
  • Minimum history: a candidate needs at least 60 months of returns before 2018-01-01 to be scored (listed on or before 2012-12-31). Anything with less than 120 months in total is flagged "short history" in every table. Instruments listed after 2012 (for example Shopify 2015, Ferrari 2016, Alibaba 2014, SGOV 2020, DBMF 2019) are excluded from the shortlist and may only appear as "not testable" notes.
  • Monthly total returns (dividends and coupons reinvested, Yahoo adjusted close) for all metrics; daily prices for the technical signals and for the walk-forward optimizer.

Computed monthly from daily USD total-return prices up to the prior month end.

Validation (pre-registered): within each position type's long list, each month rank by the signal and hold the top tercile equal-weighted versus the long-list equal-weight, rebalanced monthly, 10 bps one-way cost. A signal is kept if its OOS information ratio is above zero and the t-statistic of its OOS active return is above 1.0. Signals that fail are dropped from the technical score and reported as "did not add value out of sample". The technical score is the mean percentile rank of the kept signals; if none are kept for a type, the technical weight in the rubric is redistributed to the other components pro-rata (rule fixed now).

Tie-breakers in order: smaller OOS max drawdown, smaller FX share of USD variance, lower cost. Picks are the top 3 (stocks), 4 (EM bonds) and 1-3 (other) per type after review; the next 1-2 are runners-up.

Multiple testing: with 20-40 names screened per type the best OOS Sharpe is partly luck. Each candidate reports the deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey and Lopez de Prado, N = long-list size) and t-statistics on alpha and IR, and each type reports the Spearman rank correlation between in-sample and OOS scores. A type whose in-sample ranking has no OOS predictive value (rank correlation at or below zero) is reported as such and the picks lean on the forward models and drawdown, not on the OOS ranking.

8. What drops a candidate
  1. Fewer than 60 months of history before 2018, or fails a hard screen.
  2. OOS Sharpe below its type benchmark and OOS information ratio below zero (may remain a runner-up only if the type has fewer than 5 survivors).
  3. A reviewer "fail" on a load-bearing claim (look-ahead, survivorship, missing dividends or fees, wrong currency, non-reproducible number) that the analyst cannot fix.
  4. Any number that cannot be reproduced from committed code and data.
  5. A derivative overlay is dropped if it does not raise the portfolio's OOS Sharpe or reduce its max drawdown by at least 2 percentage points net of its costs.

10. Portfolio construction rules (Phase 5, fixed now)
  • Long-only base positions; derivatives only as overlays on positions held.
  • Non-US share at least 70% (section 3).
  • Sleeve weights within +/-10 percentage points of 55% stocks / 35% bonds / 10% other; any drift beyond the tolerance must be justified in writing and the guideline portfolio shown alongside.
  • Position counts 3 US growth stocks, 3 international growth stocks, 4 EM bond funds, 1-3 other. Single stock at most 10% of the portfolio and at least 3% if held; single fund at most 15%; single "other" asset at most 10%.
  • Rebalance quarterly (also tested: semi-annual, annual, monthly). Estimation window 3 years of daily data (also tested: 5 years). Ledoit-Wolf covariance and shrunk means as in portfolio/optimize.py.
  • Costs: 5 bps one-way on US-listed ETFs and stocks, 15 bps on foreign local shares, 0 bps on open-end funds (their fee is in the NAV); option overlays pay half the bid-ask spread, set to 1% of premium, plus $0.65 per contract; FX forwards 5 bps per roll.
  • Guideline portfolio for comparison: exact 55/35/10, equal weight inside each sleeve, same rebalance schedule.

12. Evidence standards (binding on every subagent)
  • Out of sample means the number was not used to pick the method, the shortlist or the weights. In-sample and OOS are always shown side by side.
  • Signals and weights at date t use data to t-1 only; fundamentals are lagged to their filing date.
  • Total-return series net of fund fees and the trading costs above.
  • Survivorship: the faded-leaders list is screened with the same rules, and the report shows how it did.
  • Deflated Sharpe or t-stats on every ranking table.
  • No conclusion from less than 60 months; under 120 months flagged.
  • Every number reproducible from committed code and data with a stated command; every web fact carries a source link and date.
  • Synthetic data is never a result.
  • "The guideline portfolio was as good as the optimized one" is a valid finding and is reported as such if found.

What these terms mean
Information ratio
How much a holding beat its benchmark per unit of the risk it took by differing from it (active return divided by tracking error). Above 0.5 is good, but it needs a t-statistic above about 2 to be believable.
t-statistic
How many standard errors an average is away from zero. Above about 2 the result is unlikely to be luck; below 1 it is indistinguishable from noise.
Out-of-sample (OOS)
Data the selection never saw. Here: Jan 2018 to Oct 2026, with every screen built only from data up to Dec 2017.

6. How the ranking was formed

  1. Rubric (section 7): percentile within type of OOS Sharpe (20), OOS IR (15), OOS max drawdown (10), consistency = share of rolling 36-month windows with IR above zero (10), diversification = mean OOS correlation with the other sleeves' benchmarks (10), inflation = 2021-12 to 2022-10 return (5), liquidity and cost = OOS average daily traded value and expense ratio (5), forward 7-year expected return (15), in-sample fundamental score (5). The technical weight (5) was redistributed because no signal was kept (section 6 result, see shortlist.md).
  2. Drop rule 8.2: OOS Sharpe below the type benchmark and OOS IR below zero. Applied before the reviewer verdicts.
  3. Reviewer verdicts: "fail" = dropped; "downgrade" = ranked after every pass in its type and only picked if the type has fewer passes than slots.
  4. Picks = the top 3 / 3 / 4 / 1-3 by that order; runners-up = the next two (rule-8.2 casualties may be runners-up only where the type has fewer than five survivors, section 13 clarification).

8. Caveats

  • Price indices. Home-market indices used for the local-currency view ignore dividends, which flatters local information ratios.
  • Point-in-time. Most international fundamentals could not be rebuilt as they stood in 2017 because the companies do not file with the SEC. They were screened on current statements and flagged.
  • One out-of-sample regime. 2018-2026 contained one long growth rally, the 2020 crash, the 2022 rate shock and a strong dollar. A different regime could rank everything differently.
  • Picks were chosen partly on out-of-sample results. The rubric uses 2018-2026 data, so the portfolio-level backtest of these 12 names is not a clean test. The walk-forward optimizer weights are out-of-sample by construction; the choice of names is not.
  • Multiple testing. With dozens of names per type the best out-of-sample Sharpe is partly luck, which is why deflated Sharpe and t-statistics are shown.

Carried from the adversarial reviews (scorecard.md)

22 of 31 international passers (including all eight shortlisted) were screened on current statements, not point-in-time, because they do not file with the SEC (method.md section 4 allowed this with a flag; the reviewer rates it the most serious weakness of the international shortlist); home indices for the local view are price indices, flattering local IRs by 1-4%/yr; the EM bond forward estimates rest on one method (the Monte Carlo is anchored to the yield drift); the model covered-call return runs 0.9%/yr above the CBOE indices OOS, which flatters covered calls but not drawdown.
